APPROACH

A clear process,
with honest reporting.

I start by understanding your store and what you want to improve. I review the relevant evidence, agree what to address, and report what I did and what the evidence can show—from the first audit to the final report.

01 / HOW I WORK

A practical process, step by step.

The five steps sit within three clear phases. Discover and Investigate make up the audit. Prioritize sets the plan. Implement and Verify cover improvement and measurement.

  1. AUDIT

    Discover

    What I do: Understand your store, audience, business goals, and current challenges.

    What you get: A shared understanding of your goals, priorities, and the audit scope.

    Timing: Discussed based on the implementation process.

    What I need from you: Your store URL, a little context on your audience, and the challenges you want to solve.

  2. AUDIT

    Investigate

    What I do: Examine relevant storefront pages, the customer journey, usability, and available technical evidence.

    What you get: Notes on what I observed, what supports each finding, and anything that needs more investigation.

    Timing: Discussed based on the implementation process.

    What I need from you: The key pages or journeys to review, context on known issues, and analytics access if relevant.

  3. PLAN

    Prioritize

    What I do: Rank findings by severity, strength of evidence, customer impact, and implementation effort.

    What you get: An ordered plan with the reasoning behind each proposed next step.

    Timing: Discussed based on the implementation process.

    What I need from you: Your constraints, preferences, and feedback on what matters most to the business.

  4. IMPROVE AND MEASURE

    Implement

    What I do: Complete the changes we agree on, where access, authorization, and scope permit.

    What you get: Changes within the agreed scope and a record of what was implemented.

    Timing: Discussed based on the implementation process.

    What I need from you: Approvals, appropriate access, and any assets or decisions needed for the agreed work.

  5. IMPROVE AND MEASURE

    Verify and report

    What I do: Test the changes I can verify and report what was fixed, what remains unresolved, and what can be measured.

    What you get: A clear report that separates completed work, open questions, and measurable observations.

    Timing: Discussed based on the implementation process.

    What I need from you: Any final review or confirmations needed to verify the agreed changes.

Timing is agreed first. We discuss the timing based on the implementation process, scope, access, and approvals before work begins.

02 / CLEAR LABELS

How I report findings.

I label each finding by what the available evidence actually supports.

Confirmed defect

A specific fault I reproduced or verified with evidence, with the observed behavior and context recorded.

Observed usability concern

A point in the visible shopping experience that may make a task harder. I describe what I saw without assuming its effect on every customer.

Potential issue needing further investigation

A signal that raises a question but is not enough to confirm a fault. I state what evidence would be needed next.

Recommendation not yet implemented

A suggested improvement that has not been made or verified. I keep recommendations separate from completed work.

Evidence before conclusions. Reports identify what I checked and what I could verify; a suggestion is not a result, and an unverified signal is not a confirmed defect.

03 / SCOPE & EVIDENCE

What I don't do.

I never present public storefront observations as proof of hidden backend problems, tracking failures, malware, or security breaches. I do not claim a change is complete unless the evidence supports that claim. If something cannot be checked with the access and evidence available, I say so.

06 / REALISTIC EXPECTATIONS

Good work takes evidence and patience.

SEO usually takes months to show meaningful movement. Conversion and email changes can produce useful signals sooner, but what can be measured depends on factors like traffic, implementation, and the measurement setup. I can explain what the available data shows; I cannot guarantee a particular outcome.

No guarantees. I will report what was changed and what can be measured without presenting an estimate or recommendation as a promised result.

08 / A FEW QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions.

How much does an audit or project cost?

Cost depends on the work and access involved. I will confirm the scope and price with you before any implementation begins.

How long will the work take?

It depends on the agreed scope, access, approvals, and what the evidence shows. We will discuss timing based on the implementation process before work begins.

What access do you need?

I will request only the access relevant to the agreed work. Store admin and analytics access may be needed for some reviews or changes; we can discuss the required permission level first.

What if you find nothing wrong?

I will say what I reviewed and report that I did not identify a confirmed defect within that scope. I will not invent a problem to fill a report.
